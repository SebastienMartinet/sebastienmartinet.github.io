Supernovae Explosions:

Dertimining conisistently nuclear uncertainties in large scale nucleosynthesis simulations

The p-process is a nucleosynthesis mechanism responsible for producing proton-rich isotopes of elements (p-nuclei) that cannot be formed via the s- or r-processes. It typically occurs in explosive stellar environments, such as supernovae, where high temperatures enable photodisintegration reactions (γ,n), (γ,p), and (γ,α) on pre-existing seed nuclei. These rare isotopes are crucial for understanding stellar explosions and galactic chemical evolution. In my work, I develop a comprehensive methodology to quantify uncertainties in nuclear reaction rates—particularly for reactions critical to the p-process—using a combination of Bayesian parameter estimation, ElasticNet regression, and SHAP-based feature importance from machine learning models. These uncertainties are then propagated through nucleosynthesis simulations, allowing for robust predictions of elemental abundances and a clearer identification of the most influential nuclear inputs.
